I ride a touring bike. I want a race bike for long rides and gran fondos,
and I've set myself a budget of €3,000 to €5,000 for the bike alone.

This post is how I'm deciding. When I've bought one, I'll add what I picked
and why.

## The bike I have

A **Koga WorldTraveller Signature**: Koga's touring bike, built to order.

- **Frame:** aluminium, built to carry racks and bags
- **Gears:** Shimano XT, a mountain-bike groupset with three chainrings
- **Brakes:** hydraulic discs
- **Handlebar:** a flat trekking bar
- **Weight:** about 20 kg, before any bags

It's made for carrying luggage across countries, and it shows on a day ride:

- **It's heavy.** Twenty kilos is more than twice a race bike.
- **The position is upright.** Comfortable, but not aggressive.
- **It isn't dynamic.** It's stable and steady, not quick to speed up or
  change direction.

## What I want

- **Comfort over long hours** matters most.
- **Electronic shifting**, if the budget allows.
- **Best value.** I don't mind whether it comes from a shop or straight from
  the brand online.

€660–810 bought separately. Shop brands like Specialized and Giant also cost
more than direct brands for the same parts, which the estimate doesn't model.

## The parts, one by one

### Frame

Every bike on my list is an **endurance** bike: a race bike with a slightly
taller front, longer wheelbase and room for wide tyres. The frames differ in
how they add comfort:

- **Specialized Roubaix:** a small suspension unit under the handlebars
  (about 20 mm of travel).
- **Trek Domane:** IsoSpeed, which lets the seat tube flex.
- **Canyon Endurace:** a seatpost designed to flex, and a cockpit you can
  adjust in width and height.
- **Giant Defy:** a flexy seatpost and handlebar.

### Wheels and tyres

Some bikes come with **carbon wheels** (both Canyons, Trek, Van Rysel),
others with **aluminium** ones and the money spent on comfort tech instead
(Roubaix, Defy). Carbon wheels are lighter; bought separately, the upgrade
costs about €700–850.

Wider tyres at lower pressure are more comfortable, and not slower: in one
lab test, 32 mm tyres rolled at least 5% more efficiently than otherwise
identical 30 mm ones. (The tyre maker paid for the test, so take the exact
number lightly.) Every bike here comes with 32 mm tyres.

The catch for Dutch winters: mudguards take up space. Check the clearance
*with* mudguards fitted, not the headline number.

### Groupset

At this price, every bike on my list has **electronic shifting**. Mechanical
versions start around €3,000, so the choice is between electronic groupsets:

- **Shimano 105 Di2:** the default on most of the list.
- **Shimano Ultegra Di2:** works the same, but lighter (the rear derailleur
  alone is 41 g lighter) and better finished. On the SLX 8 it comes with a
  power meter.
- **SRAM Rival AXS:** wireless, with smaller chainrings (46/33) and a 10-36
  cassette, so the easiest gears are easier.

### Brakes

All six bikes have **hydraulic disc brakes**, the same kind as my Koga. They
stop well in the wet and need little adjusting. Nothing to choose here.

### Online or a shop?

Brands that sell direct (Canyon, Decathlon's Van Rysel) give you more bike
per euro, and Canyon has a 30-day return. But you build it yourself, and
some parts are proprietary. A shop gives you a test ride, a fitting and
someone to fix it. Stock in the Netherlands is patchy right now, so it's
worth checking with a shop before relying on a web price.

## The one I lean towards

The **Canyon Endurace CF SLX 8 Di2**, because of what you get for the money.
At €4,499 it costs the same as the Specialized Roubaix SL8 Comp, but comes
with:

- **Shimano Ultegra Di2** instead of SRAM Rival AXS, one level up;
- **carbon wheels** (DT Swiss, 35 mm deep) instead of aluminium ones;
- a **power meter** (4iiii) on the cranks, according to Canyon's page;
- a claimed **8.1 kg** in size M.

Compared with the SLX 7 Di2 from the same frame family, the extra €500 buys
the Ultegra groupset, the power meter and about 340 g less weight. Same
frame, wheels and tyre clearance.

Two catches:

- It's **"coming soon"** on Canyon's site: no size has a delivery date yet.
- Its cassette is **11-34**, against 11-36 on the SLX 7, so the easiest gear
  is a little harder. Fine for the Netherlands; worth knowing for mountains.

## What's next

1. Wait for the SLX 8 Di2 to be in stock.
2. Order it online in the size Canyon recommends.
3. Ride it, and update this post.
